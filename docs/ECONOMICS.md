# Economics — AgencyOS v0.1

## 1. Principle

AgencyOS exists to operate digital assets economically, not merely to maximize content volume or vanity metrics.

## 2. North-star economic rule

**Sustainable profit per unit of capital employed governs scale.**

Views, followers, posts, videos and subscribers are intermediate indicators. Capital allocation depends on economic evidence and acceptable risk.

## 3. Cost attribution

The platform must be able to attribute direct and shared costs, including where applicable:

- model usage;
- research;
- voice;
- image/video generation;
- rendering;
- storage;
- API and connector costs;
- orchestration;
- infrastructure;
- paid tools;
- human review when used.

## 4. Revenue attribution

Revenue should be attributable, where technically possible, to:

- asset;
- agency;
- content item;
- topic/series;
- platform;
- monetization model.

## 5. Core measures

The economic layer should support, at minimum:

- cost per content item;
- revenue per content item;
- gross and contribution margin;
- agency profit;
- portfolio profit;
- ROI;
- payback;
- unit economics;
- capital invested;
- budget utilization;
- lifetime revenue where measurable.

## 6. Scaling logic

Scaling decisions should combine:

- repeatable positive economics;
- operational stability;
- sufficient demand/content supply;
- acceptable policy and platform risk;
- confidence that additional capital can create additional value.

## 7. Budget controls

Every agency must have explicit budget constraints. Limits may include daily, monthly, per-job, per-content or per-experiment ceilings. Exceeding a threshold must trigger governed behavior rather than silent overspend.

## 8. Recovery and kill criteria

Persistent negative economics may move an agency into `RECOVERY`, `PAUSED` or `RETIRING`. Kill criteria should be established in Agency DNA rather than invented only after losses occur.

## 9. Open decisions

Exact accounting method, revenue recognition, shared-cost allocation and financial data sources remain implementation decisions for later phases.
